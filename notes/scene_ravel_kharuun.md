# THE FIRST MEETING — RAVEL & A THAL KHARUUN

Context: Watcher Flats · Windward Edge. Establish respect without familiarity; parallel perception; uneasy recognition. No exposition; the world speaks through observation.

Ravel felt it before he saw them. Not roots—stone tightening.

The flats were quiet in the way they only became before sound returned wrong. Ash lay thin here, like a held breath. He had stopped walking without deciding to. That usually meant someone else already had.

A mammoth stood downslope, half veiled by steam. It did not move. Its eye tracked nothing. That was when Ravel understood: he was already seen.

A figure stepped into view between basalt ribs. Tall. Wrapped in layered hides burned pale by ash and sun. The ground under their feet had cracked once, long ago, and never healed.

They did not greet him. They listened. Ravel waited.

The Thal touched the stone with two fingers, then removed them immediately, as if the ground had spoken too loudly.

“You’re late,” the Thal said at last.

Ravel frowned. “To what?”

The Thal tilted their head, considering. “To hearing it early.”

Ravel felt the roots below twist—not alarmed, not urgent. Curious.

“You hear stone,” he said.

The Thal’s eyes lifted to him for the first time. Sharp. Measuring. Not hostile.

“And you,” they replied, “listen where it still grows.”

Neither smiled.

A tremor passed beneath them—not an earthquake, just a decision reconsidered.

The mammoth shifted its weight. Steam curled around its tusks and dissipated.

“You shouldn’t stand there,” the Thal said, gesturing slightly.

Ravel stepped aside without asking why.

A heartbeat later, a thin seam of ground collapsed where he’d been, swallowing ash and silence together.

Ravel nodded once. “Thank you.”

The Thal shook their head. “The stone didn’t mean to harm you. It just doesn’t remember you yet.”

They both turned toward the Watcher. Smoke rose steadily, not violently. Not yet.

Ravel spoke quietly. “It’s restless.”

The Thal corrected him. “It’s finished waiting.”

That earned a pause.

Ravel closed his eyes. The roots below the flats tightened, bracing. Not fleeing. Preparing.

“When?” he asked.

The Thal considered the slope of the land, the angle of the steam, the way the mammoth refused to step forward.

“Soon enough that the City will lie,” they said. “Late enough that it will sound calm when it does.”

Ravel opened his eyes.

“Will you warn them?”

The Thal laughed once—short, dry. “They don’t listen to stone.”

“And they barely listen to roots,” Ravel said.

The Thal studied him again, longer this time.

“Then stand where you can be felt,” they said. “It’s the only language left.”

Ravel nodded. The mammoth turned away, satisfied.

As the Thal stepped back into the steam, they added, almost as an afterthought:

“When the ground starts speaking loudly—don’t argue with it.”

Ravel watched the ash settle where they had been. The roots below whispered approval.

## Why This Matters (Design Notes)
- No alliance; no prophecy; mutual recognition without hierarchy.
- Stone and root listening are parallel, not superior.
- The Watcher is framed as decision, not villain.
- Establishes why Ravel respects the Thal, why the Thal tolerate Mystics, and why neither believes the City can be warned in time.
